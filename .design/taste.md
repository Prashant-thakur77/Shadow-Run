# Taste profile

## Personality
IS
- **Forensic.** Evidence first: every figure carries its source, unit and time. Looks like an exhibit, not an ad.
- **Precise.** Hairlines, a strict grid, mono numerals, exact alignments. Motion that settles to a value instead of floating.
- **Unflinching.** Shows the wreck plainly, in the clone, and shows prod not moving. Large, blunt type for the verdicts.

NEVER
- **Hacker cliché.** No green rain, glitch for its own sake, skulls, terminals pretending to type.
- **Hype SaaS.** No gradient blobs, glass cards, floating 3D shapes, "10x" claims, purple-blue.
- **Playful.** No bounce, wobble, emoji, mascots, confetti.

## Audience taste
Engineers and judges who trust Stripe docs, Linear's precision, Vercel's restraint, incident write-ups and lab reports.
They distrust marketing numbers. A visible source line under a number reads as more exciting to them than an animation.

## Brand anchors
- Near-black green ground (#0a0c0b), paper ink (#f1efe7): keep.
- Good #3ddba9, bad #ff5a45, caution #eec84e: keep, used only as meaning, never as decoration.
- Archivo condensed display, Geist text, Geist Mono numerals, Doto dot-matrix for instrument readouts: keep.
- Hairline frames, bracketed labels "[ RESULTS ]", "fig. n" source lines: keep and make them the system.
- Copy voice: plain, short sentences, from the user's side, no hype.

## Feel in motion
Mechanical and metered. Needles and counters settle with a firm ease-out (about 600-900 ms, cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)).
Breakage is a hard cut or a 120-200 ms snap, never a slow morph. Prod stays perfectly still while the clone moves.
Scroll reveals once; nothing loops forever except a slow instrument idle.

## Material and texture
Matte instrument panel, graph paper grid, phosphor readouts, carbon-copy receipts, red tamper tape, rubber stamps.

## Taste anchors
- Crash-test footage: the sled, the marked dummy, the measured frame. The wreck is the point, and it's measured.
- NTSB and incident reports: exhibits numbered, sources cited, sober type.
- Teenage Engineering product pages: instruments as graphics, exact labels, joy in precision.
- Swiss technical posters: grid, scale contrast, one red.

## Lines not to cross
- No number that isn't read from a file or the API.
- No fake UI, no mockup screens, no stock photos of people at laptops.
- Nothing essential behind hover or animation; content readable on first paint.
- The red of "bad" never used for decoration.
